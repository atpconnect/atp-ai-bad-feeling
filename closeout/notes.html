How this was made

Five rooms, one picture, ten minutes

Your own script, not the model’s — just prompts.

  • Every station was recorded to its own meeting, hosted by one person
    Not by the pilot running the room.
  • Ending the meeting is what starts the transcript
    Teams generates a transcript 2.5 to 5 minutes after a meeting is ended, not left.
  • The model was given the transcripts and a written brief, and asked for data
    Not for a deck.
  • The slides you are looking at were generated by code, not by the model
    Order, branding, navigation, this screen, the QR code: all deterministic.
  • Two models ran at once, and it was not a race
    A fast standby answered the same question in parallel.
  • There was always a deck
    A version of this file was built days ago from the question lists alone.

Leave them with: The AI did the reading. Everything that had one correct answer was done by code.

Infrastructure and integration with third-party tools

This station shows how third-party AI tools behave once they leave the sales demo. Contract terms mean one thing in the room and another thing in production, and vendors change systems without telling you.

  • Demos run on clean data, production doesn't
    The demo runs on curated conditions: twenty short English calls, a vendor's own sample documents, a small indexed folder.
  • A seat or a rate limit doesn't mean what you think it means
    The contract says seat and rate limit.
  • Vendors can change what you depend on without asking
    A vendor rolls a model version forward and outputs get shorter, quietly.
  • What it costs to leave outweighs what it cost to arrive
    Exit costs never make it into the business case: a services engagement just to get your own data back out in usable form, and the cost of…

Leave them with: The demo is the best version of the tool you'll ever see. The contract decides what you actually got, not the interface.

Swamp Planet PREVIEW — not yet run

Technical debt and data quality

This station tests a blunt claim. Most projects billed as AI projects are actually data cleanup projects wearing a different name.

  • Documentation describes an old, better behaved dataset
    The documentation lists fourteen values for a status field.
  • Almost nobody can draw where their data actually goes
    Only a small fraction of this room could sketch their own data flow end to end.
  • Ownership of the worst data usually belongs to nobody
    The worst data usually has no owner.
  • A quiet wrong answer never pages anyone
    An outage pages someone.

Leave them with: If nobody can say who owns the data, or who would notice it's wrong, that's the actual project. It comes before the model.

Ice Planet PREVIEW — not yet run

Use cases, costs, and scope creep

This station is built on one piece of arithmetic: how many AI use cases a company starts against how many ever reach production, and where that gap comes from.

  • Projects rarely get killed, they go quiet
    Most stalls aren't a budget call, a data problem, or a legal block.
  • A stalled project still has a monthly bill
    Unused development environments.
  • Scope grows without the budget ever reopening
    A tool gets approved for one team.
  • What shipped and stayed had a narrow, honest scope
    What survived was boring: a single owner, a single workflow, a metric agreed before anyone built anything.

Leave them with: The gap between started and shipped isn't a model problem. It's a nobody decided problem.

Snow Monster Cave PREVIEW — not yet run

Development

This station asks what tested actually means for an AI feature, and what happens when something passes every test the team had and still breaks in front of a customer.

  • Tested meant wildly different things from team to team
    One team's tested was an honest vibe check.
  • Passing every test didn't stop production from breaking
    A shortened prompt silently dropped a required disclaimer.
  • On call for the AI feature is usually just whoever built it
    There's almost no formal on call rotation.
  • Rolling back fixes the next answer, not the ones already sent
    A rollback can undo a prompt or a pinned model version.

Leave them with: A test suite proves a feature behaved once. It says nothing about who's holding it at two in the morning.

Asteroid Field PREVIEW — not yet run

Compliance, legal, and security

This station is built around the questions Legal, security, and compliance actually ask about an AI system, and how often the honest answer in the room is nobody yet.

  • Basic legal questions have no owner or answer
    Does a deletion request actually remove data from a trained model?
  • Review arriving late costs more than the review itself
    A use case gets built for months before a consent or bias audit requirement kills it outright.
  • Security reviews built for shipped software don't fit a service that changes itself
    One review stalls for months because its checklist assumes versioned releases.
  • Unsanctioned AI use is already carrying real data
    Logs turn up dozens of tools in active use against only a few approved ones.

Leave them with: The honest answer to who signs off on this is usually nobody yet. That vacuum stalls more work than any technical limit does.

Our own bad feeling

What that discipline caught, in this deck, this week

Your own script, not the model’s — just prompts.

  • A bug that would have failed live, in this room, tonight
    The prompt was passed as a shell argument.
  • The model could quietly promote a failed station to a real one
    Which deleted the on-screen warning that exists to prevent precisely that.
  • A 26-character overrun threw away an entire run
    Validation could not tell the difference between output that was wrong and output that was merely too long.
  • Two models running in parallel were running in sequence
    And producing perfect output the whole time.
  • The QR codes rendered as perfectly sized blank squares
    Right dimensions, right position, nothing inside.
  • A narrator that changed 9% of the words and passed every check
    Correcting one failure produced its exact opposite.

Leave them with: Every one of these was found by running the thing. Not one was found by reading it.
